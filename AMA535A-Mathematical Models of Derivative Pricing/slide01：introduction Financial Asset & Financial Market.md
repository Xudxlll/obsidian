# Financial Asset

# Types of Financial Assets

# Debt

Debt instruments are issued by entities (firms, governments, households) that borrow money. Examples include corporate bonds, government bonds, mortgages, and consumer loans. These are also called fixed-income instruments because they promise to pay a fixed amount of cash in the future.

# Equity

Equity represents ownership in a firm. Equity securities, such as common stocks or shares, are bought and sold in stock markets. Shareholders may receive dividends and have voting rights.

# Types of Financial Assets

# Derivative

A derivative is a financial instrument whose value is derived from the price of one or more underlying assets (e.g., stocks, bonds, currencies, commodities). Derivatives are primarily used for risk management, speculation, and arbitrage.

Common derivatives include: futures contracts, forward contracts, options, and swaps.   
Derivatives are traded both on organized exchanges and overthe-counter (OTC) markets.   
Some derivatives are embedded in other securities, such as convertible bonds.

# Why and How to Use Derivatives?

• Hedging: Reduce or eliminate risk exposure.   
Speculation: Take positions to profit from expected market movements.   
• Arbitrage: Lock in risk-free profits by exploiting price discrepancies.   
Asset transformation: Change the risk/return profile of a portfolio without buying or selling the underlying assets.

# Summary

Financial assets include debt, equity, and derivatives.   
Financial markets provide platforms for trading these assets and for price discovery.   
Derivatives play a crucial role in risk management, speculation, and market efficiency.
