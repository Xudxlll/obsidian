(a) Holding a stock;   
(b) Holding a house;   
(c) Holding an insurance contract;   
(d) Shorting a stock; and   
(e) Paying a mortgage.

# Solution.

The values are

(a) stock price;   
(b) house price;   
(c) insurance contract price;   
(d) negative stock price; and   
(e) negative the total present value of the mortgage.

3. Determine which of the following situations must lead to an arbitrage opportunity.

(a) There exists a portfolio whose current value is non-positive and future value is non-negative for sure;   
(b) There exists a portfolio whose current value is positive and future value is zero for sure;   
(c) There exists a portfolio whose current value is positive and future value is negative for sure; and   
(d) There exists a portfolio whose current value is negative and future value may be zero or positive.

# Solution.

(a) No. The value may be zero all the time.   
(b) Yes. Call the portfolio A. Consider a portfolio B: short in A and long in cash $\varepsilon = \Pi _ { A } ( t ) > 0$ . Then at the present time t

$$
\Pi_ {B} (t) = - \Pi_ {A} (t) + \varepsilon = 0.
$$

At maturity T ,

$$
\Pi_ {B} (T) = - \Pi_ {A} (T) + \varepsilon = \varepsilon > 0.
$$

(c) Yes. Similar to (b).   
(d) Yes. Similar to (b).

4. A bank quotes you an annual interest rate of 14% with quarterly compounding. What is the equivalent rate with (a) continuous compounding, and (b) annual compounding (e!ective rate)?

# Solution.

(a) The rate with continuous compounding is

$$
4 \ln \left(1 + \frac {0 . 1 4}{4}\right) = 0. 1 3 7 6
$$

or 13.76% per annum. (b) The rate with annual compounding is

$$
\left(1 + \frac {0 . 1 4}{4}\right) ^ {4} - 1 = 0. 1 4 7 5
$$

or 14.75% per annum.

5. An investor will receive \$1, 100 in one year in return for an investment of \$1, 000 now. Calculate the percentage return per annum with (a) annual compounding, (b) semiannual compounding, (c) monthly compounding, and (d) continuous compounding.

# Solution.

(a) With annual compounding the return is

$$
\frac {1 1 0 0}{1 0 0 0} - 1 = 0. 1
$$

or 10% per annum. (b) With semi-annual compounding the return is R where

$$
1 0 0 0 \left(1 + \frac {R}{2}\right) ^ {2} = 1 1 0 0
$$

i.e.,

$$
1 + \frac {R}{2} = \sqrt {1 . 1} = 1. 0 4 8 8
$$

so that $R = 0 . 0 9 7 6$ . The percentage return is therefore 9.76% per annum. (c) With monthly compounding the return is R, where

$$
1 0 0 0 \left(1 + \frac {R}{1 2}\right) ^ {1 2} = 1 1 0 0
$$

i.e.,

$$
1 + \frac {R}{1 2} = \sqrt [ 1 2 ]{1 . 1} = 1. 0 0 7 9 7
$$

so that $R = 0 . 0 9 5 7$ . The percentage return is therefore 9.57% per annum. (d) With continuous compounding the return is R where:

$$
1 0 0 0 e ^ {R} = 1 1 0 0
$$

i.e.,

$$
e ^ {R} = 1. 1
$$

so that $R = \ln ( 1 . 1 ) = 0 . 0 9 5 3$ . The percentage return is therefore 9.53% per annum.

6. A deposit account pays annual interest rate of 12% with continuous compounding, but the interest is actually paid quarterly. How much interest will be paid each quarter on a \$10, 000 deposit?

# Solution.

$$
e ^ {0. 1 2} = \left(1 + \frac {R}{4}\right) ^ {4}

$$
