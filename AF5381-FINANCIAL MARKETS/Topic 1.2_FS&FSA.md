## Slide 7

Income Statement
It shows the amount of money made due to firm’s operation during a particular time span.
Revenue – Expenses = Income
Analogously, view the I/S as the result of financial manager’s decisions.
“Top half” (from Sales to EBIT) reflects the investment decisions.
“Lower half” (from EBIT to NI and EPS) reflects the financing decisions.
Owing to the realization principle, the figures shown may not be the actual cash inflows and outflows that occurred during the period.
Profit should be realized when goods are transferred or risk and rewards are transferred.
Revenue is recognized at the time of sale (not time of collection).
The unrealized appreciation of property owned will not be recognized as income until it is sold (providing an opportunity for income smoothing).
Expenses associated with the sale would be recognized at the same time (GAAP’s matching principle).
Non-cash Items
I/S contains non-cash items that make it different from cash flow.
“Depreciation allowance” is the most important non-cash item.
Deferred tax” is another non-cash item shown on the B/S as deferred tax liability.

## Slide 8

Cash Flow of the Firm (CFA)
An important piece of financial information but not available in the financial statements.
The statement of cash flows helps to explain the change in accounting cash and equivalents.
Finance perspective is more interested in firm’s ability to generate cash flow (that determines firm value).
Two methods to estimate and they are equivalent:
Direct method
CF(A) = Cash Flow to Creditors (CF(B))
+ Cash Flow to Owners (CF(S))
CF(B) = Interest - Net New L-T Bonds
CF(S) = Dividends – Net New Equity Raised

## Slide 9

Cash Flow of the Firm (CFA)
Indirect method
CF(A) = Operating Cash Flow (OCF)
– Changes in Fixed Assets (∆FA) 				 – Additions to NWC (∆NWC)
CF(A) involves three components:
OCF
Results from the firm’s day-to-day activities of producing and selling.
OCF = EBIT – tc*(EBIT – Interest) + Depreciation
= EBIT – Tax + Depreciation
∆FA
It is the net spending on fixed assets (i.e. purchase – sales).
FA = Changes in Fixed Assets + Depreciation
∆NWC
Amount spent on NWC (i.e. CA – CL)
NWC = End NWC - Beginning NWC

II. Ratio Analysis
To facilitate comparisons of firms of different size, or the same firm at different points in time. There are 2 approaches.
Standardized Financial Statements
Use percentage to eliminate the size effect
B/S items as %age of total assets
I/S items as %age of total sales
Ratio Analysis
Two ways to compare
Cross-sectional comparison
time-series comparison (trend analysis)

## Slide 14

II. Ratio Analysis
Five major groups of ratios:
Liquidity ratios to measure short-term solvency
Leverage ratios to measure long-term solvency
Turnover ratios to measure asset management efficiency
Profitability ratios to measure operation efficiency
Market value ratios to measure firm value (not from financial statements)

## Slide 15

Liquidity Ratios
They measure a firm’s ability to meet cash needs as they arise.
Current Ratio = CA/CL = $708/$540 = 1.31 times.
Quick (Acid-test) Ratio = (CA – Inv)/CL = $(708 – 422)/$540 = 0.53 times.
Cash Ratio = Cash/CL = 0.18 times.

## Slide 16

Leverage Ratios
They measure a firm’s level of indebtedness, and its ability to service debt.
Total Debt Ratio = TD/TA = (TA – TE)/TA = 0.28 times
Debt Equity Ratio = TD/TE = 0.38 times.
Equity Multiplier = TA/TE = 1.38 times (= 1 + DE ratio)
(All these 3 ratios are inter-related. One implies the other two.)
Times Interest Earned Ratio = EBIT/Interest = 4.26 times.
Cash Coverage Ratio = (EBIT + Dep + Amort)/Interest = 6.21 times.

## Slide 17

Turnover Ratios (Asset Management Measures)
They measure the liquidity of specific assets and the efficiency of managing assets.
Inventory Turnover = COGS/Inventory = 3.4 times.
Days’ Sales in Inventory = 365/Inventory Turnover = 107.34 days.
Receivables Turnover = Sales/(A/R) = 12.29 times.
Day’s Sales in Receivables (Average Collection Period) = 365 days/Receivables Turnover = 29.69 days.
Total Asset Turnover = Sales/TA = 0.64 times.

## Slide 18

Profitability Ratios
They measure the overall performance of a firm and its efficiency in managing assets, liabilities, and equity.
Profit Margin = NI/Sales = 15.69%.
EBITDA Margin = EBITDA/Sales = 37.91%.
It looks more directly at OCF than NI does.
It does not include the effect of capital structure or taxes.
Return on (Book) Assets (ROA) = NI/TA = 10.11%.
Return on (Book) Equity (ROE) = NT/TE = 13.99%.
Also called Return on Net Worth.

## Slide 19

Market Value Ratios
These measures combine accounting and stock market data.
Price-Earnings Ratio (p/e Ratio) = Price/Earnings per Share (EPS) = 8.01 times.
Trailing PE Ratio = Current Stock Price / Past 12-month EPS.
PEG Ratio = PE / (Expected future earnings growth rate x 100)
E.g. If expected growth rate in EPS = 6%, PEG ratio = 8.01 / 6 = 1.33.
Cyclical Adjusted PE Ratio (CAPE) = Current Stock Price / Average of the previous 10 years of EPS.
Price-Sales Ratio = Price / Sales per share = $88 / $70 = 1.26 times.

## Slide 20

Market Value Ratios
These measures combine accounting and stock market data.
Market-to-Book Ratio = MV(Equity)/BV(Equity) = 1.12 times.
Market Capitalization = Stock Price x Shares Outstanding = $2.9 trillion.
Apple’s market cap = USD3.38 trillion.
Enterprise Value, EV = Mkt Cap + MV(interest-bearing debt) – Cash = $3.459 trillion.
It estimates how much it would take to buy all of the outstanding stock of a firm and also to pay off the debt.
If we were a buyer, the cash could be used immediately to buy back debt of pay a dividend.
EV Multiple = EV/EBITDA = 3.95 times.
It is especially useful for firm comparison as it is not directly affected by capital structure, taxes, or capital spending.

## Slide 21

The Dupont Identity
ROE = (Net Income/Sales) x (Sales/Assets) x (Assets/Equity).
= Profit margin x Total Asset Turnover x Equity Multiplier
= Operating Efficiency x Asset Use Efficiency x Financial Leverage
= 0.1569 x 0.64 x 1.38 = 13.99%.
Dividend Payout Ratio = Cash Dividends / NI
= 33.33%.
Retention / Plowback Ratio = ∆RE / NI
= 1 – Dividend Payout Ratio
= 66.67%.

## Slide 22

Financing and Growth
A firm’s ability to grow depends on its financing policies.
The maximum growth rate that can be achieved with no external financing (i.e. only through internal financing), is called internal growth rate:
IGR = (ROA x Retention Rate) / (1 – ROA x Retention Rate)
= (10.11% x 66.67%) / ( 1 – 10.11% x 66.67%)
= 7.22%
The maximum growth rate that can be achieved with no external equity financing (i.e. maintaining the same D/E ratio) is called sustainable growth rate:
SGR = (ROE x Retention Rate) / (1 – ROE x Retention Rate)
= (13.99% x 66.67%) / ( 1 – 13.99% x 66.67%)
= 10.28%.

## Slide 23

Financing and Growth
Determinants of Growth
From the Du Pont identity, a firm’s ability to sustain growth depends on:
Profit margin (operating efficiency)
Dividend policy (retention rate)
Financial policy (debt-equity ratio)
Total asset turnover (asset use efficiency)

## Slide 24

Problems with FSA
There is no underlying theory to identify which quantities to look at and to guide setting the benchmarks.
Financial statements do not reflect value and risk.
It is hard to compare conglomerates which own more or less unrelated lines of business.
Firms in different countries are hard to compare due to different accounting standards.
Other difficulties in valid comparison such as
Different accounting procedures on, say, inventory
Different firms may have different fiscal years
Firms with high seasonality in businesses
Transient events such as one-time profit may affect financial performance.
