Types of Market (based on order trading)
Order-Driven System
A limit-order book stores all orders that become public knowledge.
Viewing all standing orders, a trader knows what trades will be executed if he enters a new limit order.
Example: Tokyo, Paris, HK
Advantages of Order-Driven System
Transparency
Public limit order book is informative and “best execution” can be achieved.

## Slide 24

Market Microstructure
Types of Market (based on order trading)
Quote-driven System
It only displays bids and asks of designated market makers and specialists for the specific security that is being traded.
Example: NYSE
Advantages of Quote-Driven System
Efficiency
Automation improves the speed and cost of order processing.
Market Making
Dealers maintain an orderly and liquid market for thinly traded stocks.
Anonymity
The limit order book is the private knowledge of the specialist.
Better execution with a lower effective bid-ask spread.

## Slide 25

Market Microstructure
Types of Markets (based on order matching)
Direct search market
Buyers and sellers must seek each other out directly.
e.g. sale of a used refrigerator
Brokered market
Trading is sufficiently large to make brokers profitable to offer search services to buyers and sellers.
e.g. the real estate market
e.g. a brokered market for large block transactions
e.g. in the primary market, investment bankers act as brokers.
Dealer market
Dealers, unlike brokers, trade assets for their own accounts.
The dealer’s profit market is the “bid-asked” spread.
e.g. NASDAQ
Auction Market
All transactors in a good converge at one place to bid on or offer a good.
An advantage over dealer markets is that one need not search to find the best price for a good and thus save the bid-asked spread.
Periodic auction (e.g. in the art world) vs continuous auction (e.g. NYSE)

## Slide 36

Market Failure
Mortgage Derivatives
Collateralized debt obligations (CDOs)
Mortgage pool divided into slices or tranches to concentrate default risk
Senior tranches: Lower risk, highest rating
Junior tranches: High risk, low or junk rating
The earliest CDOs were constructed in 1987 by the former investment bank Drexel Burnham Lambert headed by Michael Milken.
To create a CDO, investment banks gather cash flow-generating assets—such as mortgages, bonds, and other types of debt—and repackage them into discrete classes, or tranches based on the level of credit risk assumed by the investor.
For example
mortgage-backed securities (MBS) are comprised of mortgage loans,
asset-backed securities (ABS) contain corporate debt, auto loans, or credit card debt.
MBS and ABS
MBS are called pass-throughs because the cash flows produced by homeowners paying off their mortgages are passed through to investors.
Most were issued by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac which got taken over by the federal government in 2008.

## Slide 37

Market Failure
Mortgage Derivatives
Credit Default Swap (CDS)
A CDS is an insurance contract against the default of the borrower
Investors bought sub-prime loans and used CDS to insure their safety
Some big swap issuers did not have enough capital to back their CDS when the market collapsed.
CDS traded mostly “over the counter”, so less transparent, no posted margin requirements
Consequence: CDO insurance failed
