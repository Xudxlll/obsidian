                                    The Influence of CEO Financial Background on Corporate Financialization

                                                                                Abstract
                        Entity enterprises are increasingly engaging in financial activities to enhance financial capital
                        allocation.  As  core  decision-makers,  CEOs              significantly  impact  the  financialization  of  these
                        enterprises. This paper, grounded in Top Echelon theory and Imprinting theory, examines the
                        effect of a CEO's financial background on corporate financialization. Utilizing data from A-share
                        listed companies from 2008 to 2022, the study aims to determine the direction and magnitude of
                        this influence and explore the mediating roles and heterogeneity of these effects. Key findings
                        include: (1) CEOs with a financial background significantly promote corporate financialization; (2)
                        this  promotion  occurs  primarily  through  increased  managerial  overconfidence  and  alleviated
                        corporate financing constraints; and (3) the impact is more pronounced in non-state-owned and
                        low-cash-flow firms compared to state-owned and high-cash-flow firms. Based on these insights,
                        the paper suggests targeted management strategies.
                        Keywords:        CEO  financial  background;          corporate     financialization;    overconfidence;  financing
                        constraints

                        1. Introduction

                               The background characteristics of           business leaders, particularly their financial background,
                        profoundly  impact  the  strategic  decision-making  and  operational  performance  of  enterprises
                        (Oradi et al., 2020). As the core decision-maker of an enterprise, a CEO's financial background not
                        only  represents  their  professional  ability  and  experience  but  also  influences  the  enterprise's
                        behavior and performance in financial markets (Liu et al., 2020). Understanding the significance
                        of  a  CEO's  financial  background  is  crucial  for  comprehending  corporate                       decision-making
                        mechanisms, optimizing corporate governance structures, and enhancing market competitiveness.
                        CEOs with a financial background possess profound knowledge of financial theory and rich
                        practical experience, enabling them to make more informed and precise decisions when faced with
                        complex financial problems. These CEOs can better grasp dynamic financial market changes,
                        identify potential risks and opportunities, and thereby create more value for the enterprise (Yang et
                        al., 2021). Furthermore, a financial background helps CEOs improve the financial management
                        level and operational efficiency of the enterprise, focusing on capital liquidity, cost control, and
                        capital   allocation   to   manage   financial   resources   more   effectively   and   enhance   market
                        competitiveness.
                               The financialization of entity enterprises refers to the increasing participation of enterprises
                        in financial market activities and the strategic use of financial capital (Liu et al., 2022). This trend
                        reflects a growing understanding and utilization of financial capital by enterprises and the closer
                        integration between finance and the real economy. By engaging in financial market activities,
                        enterprises   can   leverage  financial   mechanisms   to   achieve   capital   appreciation   and   risk
                        diversification.  This  financialization  also  promotes  innovation  and  development  within  the
                        financial market, fostering a positive interaction between financial capital and the real economy
                        (Li et al., 2021). However, the financialization of entity enterprises also introduces risks and
                        challenges,  such  as  balancing  financial  investment  with  core  business  activities,  controlling
                        financial risks, and maintaining sound enterprise operations. Addressing these challenges requires

in-depth  analysis  and  effective  responses  from             business  leaders  (Song  &  Wu,  2022).  This
necessitates  a  thorough  investigation  into  how  a  CEO's  financial  background  impacts  the
financialization of entity enterprises, providing valuable insights and references for corporate
decision-makers.
      This   study   aims   to   explore   the   impact   of   a   CEO's   financial   background   on   the
financialization of entity enterprises. Specifically, it uses panel data from A              -share listed firms from
2008 to 2022 to conduct an empirical analysis based on a double fixed                     -effects model to test the
causal  relationship  between  a  CEO's  financial  background  and  the  financialization  of  entity
enterprises. The study also explores potential mediating roles of managerial overconfidence and
financing constraints in this relationship, examining the mechanisms through which a CEO's
financial   background   influences   enterprise   financialization.   Additionally,   it   analyzes   the
heterogeneity of these effects from the perspectives of enterprise nature and cash flow, providing
comprehensive insights into real-world complexities. Based on the empirical results, the paper
proposes targeted development strategies.
      The contribution of this paper is multi          -faceted. First, it addresses a relative gap in current
research  on  executive  financial  backgrounds,  which  often  fo                  cuses  narrowly  on  banking
backgrounds without a holistic view of executive financial backgrounds (Li et al., 2017). This
paper fills this gap, enriching the research on executive financial backgrounds and offering new
perspectives and ideas. Second, it refines existing research by focusing specifically on CEOs, who
play a decisive role in firms, rather than generalizing across all executives. This focus eliminates
noise from varying managerial authorities and enhances the relevance and practicality of the
findings. Third, the paper not only examines the impact of a CEO's financial background on
corporate financialization but also reveals the underlying mechanisms and specific environments
influencing this impact. This comprehensive analysis enriches the fiel                    d of financialization and
provides practical references for enterprises. Lastly, the paper expands the application scope of
branding  theory  by  exploring  its  relevance  to  the  relationship  between  a  CEO's  financial
background and corporate financialization, thus enhancing the theoretical depth and breadth of the
study.
      The structure of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 provides a literature review,
summarizing existing research related to the topic. Section 3 details the methodology and data
used   in  the  study.  Section  4  presents  the  results  and discussion,  offering  an  analysis  of  the
empirical findings. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper, summarizing the main insights and
proposing future research directions.

2. Literature review and research hypothesis

      Top echelon theory emphasizes the significant impact of the traits and background of a firm's
top management team (TMT) on the firm's strategic decisions and performance (Hambrick and
Mason, 2004) Among them, the CEO, as a core member of the to                           p management team, has a
non-negligible role to play in the development of the enterprise in terms of his or her traits and
background characteristics. Especially in the context of financialization of entity enterprises, the
impact of CEO's financial background on the process of financialization of firms becomes more
and more prominent (Hambrick, 2007). First of all, in the process of financialization of entity
enterprises, a large number of financial activities are involved, such as financing, investment,                     risk

===== PDF第5页 =====
management, etc., which all require professional financial knowledge and skills. Therefore, CEOs
with  financial  backgrounds  can  better  grasp  the  dynamics  of  the  financial  market,  identify
potential risks and opportunities, and provide strong support for the financialization process of
enterprises (Carpenter et al., 2004). Second, the CEO's financial background helps to enhance the
financialization ability of the enterprise, which refers to the enterprise's ability to use the financial
market  for  resource  allocation  and  risk  management.  CEOs  with  financial  backgrounds  can
establish  closer  cooperative  relationships  with  financial  institutions,  obtain  more  financial
resources and information support, and further promote the financialization process of enterprises
(Wang et al., 2016). In addition, the financial background of the CEO also helps optimize the
financialization  strategy  of  the  enterprise.  Financialization  strategy  is  the  financialization
development  goal  and  action  plan  formulated  by  the  enterprise  according  to  the  market
environment  and  the  enterprise's  conditions  (Waldman  et  al.,  2004).  CEOs  with  a  financial
background can more accurately grasp the changing trends of the financial market, combine them
with  the  actual  situation  of  the  enterprise,          and  make  comprehensive  consideration  of  the
enterprise's resource allocation, risk control, and other aspects from the perspective of finance to
ensure that the enterprise maintains a sound development trend in the process of financialization
(Whitler et al., 2021).
      Imprinting theory has its roots in biology. Both individuals and organizations have several
environmentally  sensitive  periods  in  their  developmental  process  (Patten  et  al.,  2014).  The
environmental characteristics of a particular phase will have a significant impact on the individual
or organization. Individuals and organizations will be imprinted with environmental characteristics,
and this imprint will continue to affect the behavior of individuals and organizations, and will not
disappear easily (Marquis and Tilcsik, 2013). The financial background of a CEO leaves a deep
imprint on his or her career, which may originate from his or her early work experience in a
financial institution or through the systematic study of financial literacy and practical experience
accumulation (Mathias et al., 2015)  These experiences give CEOs an in-depth understanding
and perception of financial markets, financial instruments, and risk management, forming a unique
financial cognitive stigma. On the one hand, CEOs' financial background stigma influences their
affective tendencies and risk preferences. CEOs with financial backgrounds usually hold a more
open and positive attitude toward the financial market and are more willing to try new financial
instruments and strategies (Shan et al., 2024). At the same time, they also tend to be more cautious
and robust in risk control. This emotional stigma and risk preference stigma enable CEOs to weigh
the pros and cons more rationally in the process of promoting the financialization of entity
enterprises and formulate a financialization strategy that is in line with the actual situation of the
firm (Shan et al., 2024). On the other hand, the stigma of CEOs' financial backgrounds is also
reflected  in  their  behavioral  decisions.  CEOs  with  financial  backgrounds  can  use  financial
instruments more skillfully and conduct resource allocation and risk management more effectively
when facing financialization decisions. They can optimize the financing structure of enterprises
and  improve  the  efficiency  of  capital  use  from  a  financial  perspective,  thus  promoting  the
financialization process of entity enterprises (Cheng et al., 2023). Combined with the above
analysis, this paper proposes research hypothesis 1.

      H1: The CEO's financial backgrounds significantly contribute to the financialization of
entity enterprises.

aggressive financial investment strategies and pursue higher financial returns, thus accelerating the
financialization  process  of  entity  enterprises  (Zhong  and  Yan,  2024).  Second,  the  financial
background of CEOs helps them to establish                  extensive financial networks and resources.CEOs
have access to more financial resources and information support, which further enhances their

      H2: The CEO’s financial background significantly contributes to the financialization of
entity enterprises by increasing managerial overconfidence.

      The    CEO's  financial  background           brings  professional  financial  knowledge  and  practical
experience to the enterprise, thus effectively alleviating the enterprise financing constraints. In a
modern  market  economy,  financing  constraints  are  one  of  the  key  factors  restricting  the
development of entity enterprises (Sisli, 2012). CEOs with financial backgrounds gain insight into
financing opportunities and risks through their deep theoretical knowledge of finance and rich
practical experience. They can choose appropriate financing channels and methods according to
the actual situation and financial needs of the enterprise, to ensure that the enterprise can obtain
sufficient financial support at the critical moment (Wu and Hu, 2021). In addition, CEOs with
financial backgrounds can also provide professional financing advice and guidance to enterprises,
helping them optimize the financing structure and further reduce financing costs. Secondly, the
financial background of CEOs helps enterprises establish close cooperative relationships with
financial institutions, thus alleviating financing constraints (Lun, 2022). As an important channel
for  capital  financing,  financial  institutions  play  a  crucial  role  in  the  development  of  entity
enterprises.  CEOs  with  financial  backgrounds  understand  the  operation                      mechanism  and  risk
preferences of financial institutions and can better communicate and coordinate with financial
institutions. By establishing mutual trust with financial institutions, enterprises can obtain more
financing support and preferential policies, thus effectively alleviating financing constraints (Gu et

al., 2023). In addition, the CEO's financial background helps enterprises grasp the opportunities of
the  financial  market  and  achieve  the  optimal  allocation  of  financial  assets,  thus  alleviating
financing constraints. The financial market is a market full of opportunities and challenges, and
CEOs  with  financial  backgrounds  can  discover  and  utilize  investment  opportunities  in  the
financial market through their professional financial knowledge and keen insight. They can realize
the financialized income of the enterprise by investing in financial assets such as stocks, bonds,
funds, etc., and bringing additional sources of capital for the enterprise (Zhong et al., 2023). CEOs
with financial backgrounds are also able to formulate a reasonable asset allocation plan according
to the risk tolerance and return requirements of the enterprise to achieve the optimal allocation of
financial assets and further reduce the financing costs and risks of the enterprise (Ding et al.,
2023).  In  summary,  the  financial  background  of  CEOs  effectively  alleviates  the  financing
constraints  of  enterprises  and  promotes  the  level  of  financialization  of  entity  enterprises  by
providing   professional   financial   knowledge   and   practical   experience,   establishing   close
cooperation with financial institutions, and grasping financial market opportunities. Accordingly,
research hypothesis 3 is proposed.

      H3: The financial background of CEOs significantly contributes to the financialization
of entity enterprises by easing financing constraints.

3.2 Variable definition
     The dependent variable in this paper is the level of financialization of entity enterprises,
which reflects the allocation and operation of financial assets in the business process of entity
enterprises (Zheng et al., 2019). A higher level of financialization may mean that enterprises focus
more on obtaining revenue through financial activities rather than through traditional production
and operation activities. Too high a level of financialization may mean that enterprises are exposed
to greater financial risks, which may have a greater impact on the stable operation of enterprises
once the financial market fluctuates (Xu and Guo, 2023). Especially when entity enterprises
allocate a large amount of funds to the stock market, real estate, and other financial areas to chase
short-term excess returns, the level of financialization of entity enterprises will rise, which may
lead to the hollowing out of the real economy and hurt the country's economic development (Song
and Wu, 2022). In this paper, referring to the study of Qi et al. (2021), the proportion of financial
assets to total assets is used as a proxy variable for the financialization of entity enterprises, and
the specific formula is as follows:
          FINRATIO = (Trading financial assets + Derivative financial assets + Net loans and
 advances granted + Net available-for-sale financial assets + Net held-to-maturity investments +
                                Net investment properties) / Total assets (4)
     The independent variable in this paper is CEO's financial background, which mainly refers to
the CEO's experience and engagement in financial institutions or related fields  (Lun, 2022).
Specifically, a CEO can be said to have a financial background if he or she has held positions in
policy  banks,  commercial  banks,         investment  banks,  financial  regulators,  fund  management
companies,   insurance   companies,   exchanges,   securities   firms,   securities   registration   and
settlement firms, futures firms, trust companies, investment management firms, and other financial
institutions (Oradi et al., 2020). This paper uses a dummy variable for whether the CEO has a
financial background as a proxy variable for the            CEO's financial background, which is 1 if the
CEO has a financial background and 0 if the CEO does not have a financial backg                    round (Jiang et
al., 2022).

3.3 Data sources
     The  firm-level  data  used  in  this  paper  comes  from  the  Cathay  Pacific  database.  After
excluding financial firms, ST or PT, no delisted and insolvent firms during the sample period,
24,004 valid samples are obtained, encompassing 3,919 listed firms from 2008-2022.

4.4 Heterogeneity analysis
      Entity firms are classified into state-owned and non-state-owned firms to examine how
different firm natures would make the effect of CEO's financial background on the financialization
of entity firms exhibit heterogeneity, and the regression results are shown in columns (1) and (2)
of Table 7. In the sample of state-owned enterprises, the coefficient of the impact of CEO's
financial background on the financialization of entity enterprises is 0.0049, but it does not pass the
significance test at any level; in the sample of non-state-owned enterprises, the coefficient of the
impact of     CEO's financial background             on the financialization of entity enterprises is 0.0100,
which passes the significance test at the 1% level. It can be seen that the impact of CEO's financial
background      on  the  financialization  of  entity  enterprises  is  greater  in  non-state-owned  firms
compared to the sample of state-owned firms.
      State-owned enterprises (SOEs) tend to be subject to more government intervention and
policy guidance due to their special property rights structure and business objectives (Lin et al.,
2020). Such intervention and guidance limit the CEO's autonomy in the decision-making process
to a certain extent, especially in financial investment and capital operation (Helmich and Gilroy,
2012). Therefore, even if the CEO has a rich financial background, his or her influence on the
financialization of the firm may be constrained to a certain extent. However, in non-state-owned

===== PDF第15页 =====
enterprises, CEOs have relatively greater decision-making power, and their business objectives are
more inclined to maximize economic benefits (Zhou et al., 2017). When CEOs have a financial
background, they are better able to utilize their professional knowledge and experience to grasp
market opportunities and make financial investments and capital operations. In this context, the
financial background of CEOs has a more obvious role in promoting the financialization of entity
enterprises. In addition, the financing environment faced by non-state-owned enterprises is usually
more severe, especially in obtaining bank loans and capital market financing (Tan et al., 2014).
CEOs  with  financial  backgrounds  are  more  likely  to  optimize  the  resource  allocation  of
enterprises and improve operational efficiency through financialization.
      The cash flow status of the enterprise will be more limited to the play of the CEO's financial
background      and strength, thus making the impact of the                 CEO's financial background             on the
financialization of the entity enterprise show obvious heterogeneity. Based on the mean value of
the enterprise's cash flow, entity enterprises are divided into high cash flow enterprises and low
cash flow enterprises, and regression analysis is carried out separately, and the regression results
are shown in columns (3) and (4) of Table 7. In the sample of high cash flow firms, the coefficient
of the impact of CEO's financial background on the financialization of entity firms is 0.0049, but
it does not pass the test of significance at any level; in the sample of low cash flow firms, the
coefficient of the impact of CEO's financial background on the financialization of entity firms is
0.0054, which passes the test of significance at the 1% level. It can be seen that the effect of
CEO's financial background on the financialization of entity enterprises is greater in the low cash
flow firms compared to the high cash flow firms sample.
      From the perspective of firms' financial health, high cash-flow firms                        usually have more
abundant working capital and stable capital flows, and therefore have greater flexibility and
autonomy in their financial investment decisions (Almeida et al., 2004). Low cash flow firms, on
the  other  hand,  tend  to  face  capital  shortages           and  financing  pressures,  and  their  financial
investment decisions are more likely to be constrained by their capital position (Das et al., 2013).
Against this background, the role of CEOs with a financial background in low-cash-flow firms is
particularly important. With their profound financial knowledge and experience, they can more
accurately  assess  the  firm's  capital  situation  and  market  environment,  and  formulate  a
financialization strategy that is more in line with the reality of the firm. They are not only able to
utilize the limited funds for investment more efficiently, but also able to improve the capital status
of the enterprise and alleviate the financing pressure through reasonable capital operation (Sheikh,
2022).

5. Conclusions and implications
      5.1 Conclusions
      This  paper  focuses  on  the  impact  of            CEO's  financial  background            on  entity  enterprises
financialization. The panel data of 3919 A-share listed enterprises from 2008-2022 is used to carry
out empirical analyses,        explore the causal relationship between the two, explicate mechanism,
and examine the asymmetric influence under different conditions. The main research conclusions
are  as  follows.  (1)       The  CEO's  financial  background               significantly  increases  the  level  of
financialization of entity enterprises. This effect still holds significantly after multiple robustness
tests of replacing the explanatory variables, adjusting the research window period, lagging the
dependent  variable  by  one  period,  and  PSM-DID.  (2)                       The  CEO's  financial  background
significantly increases the level of financialization of entity enterprises mainly through two paths:
increasing managerial overconfidence and alleviating corporate finance constraints. (3) The effect
of CEO's financial background on the financialization of entity enterprises is more pronounced in
non-state-owned firms and low-cash-flow firms.

      5.2 Policy implications
      The findings of this paper point out that CEO's financial background significantly increases
the level of financialization of entity enterprises. While making full use of                        the CEO's financial
background to promote the financialization of entity enterprises, enterprises need to be alert to the
risk  of  excessive  financialization.  Therefore,  it  is  suggested  that  when  selecting  CEOs  with
financial backgrounds, enterprises should emphasize their understanding of and commitment to

===== PDF第17页 =====
the development of the main business of the entity, in addition to their financial expertise. In the
decision-making  process,  CEOs  should  adequately  weigh  the  relationship  between  financial
investment and the development of the main business, and ensure that the financial activities serve
the sound development of the main business, instead of over-pursuing the financial gains and
neglecting the main business.
      Enterprises should establish a scientific decision-making mechanism to avoid CEOs making
overly risky financial investment decisions due to overconfidence. The CEO's decisions can be
supervised and checked through the establishment of a decision-making advisory committee and
the introduction of external expert consultants. At the same time, the construction of corporate
culture should be strengthened to advocate rational and sound business concepts, to reduce the
phenomenon of overconfidence from the source. In response to the mechanism of easing the role
of corporate financing constraints, enterprises should make full use of the advantages of the CEO's
financial  background,  strengthen  cooperation  with  financial  institutions,  broaden  financing
channels, and reduce financing costs. At the same time, optimize the internal financing structure of
the enterprise, improve the efficiency of capital use, and reduce the financing risk. In addition,
enterprises can also improve their credit rating among financial institutions by establishing a credit
rating system and improving the quality of information disclosure, to obtain more financing
support.
      For  non-state-owned  enterprises,  they  should  adhere  to  their  main  business  orientation,
ensure that financial activities serve the sound development of their main business, and avoid the
blind pursuit of financial gains. Enhancing the financial literacy of management will help to view
the financialization process more rationally and avoid excessive speculation. At the same time,
optimize the corporate governance structure, play the role of supervision and checks and balances,
and ensure the science and compliance of CEO decision-making. For low-cash-flow enterprises,
they should improve their cash-flow situation by optimizing financial management and improving
operational efficiency. At the same time, it actively seeks cooperation with financial institutions
and utilizes the advantage of the CEO's financial background to broaden financing channels and
alleviate financing constraints. In addition, the enterprise can also consider attracting more capital
into it by introducing strategic investors and implementing equity incentives to provide financial
support for the financialization process of the enterprise.
